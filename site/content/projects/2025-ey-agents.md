EY is one of the largest professional-services firms in the world, and every year its tax and consulting practices deliver an astonishing volume of bespoke, high-stakes work -- returns prepared, regulations interpreted, transactions structured, and clients advised across nearly every country on Earth. Almost all of it leans on the hard-won judgment of individual experts, which means the same problems get solved from scratch again and again. The expertise is real and deep, but much of it lives in people's heads and in scattered documents rather than in systems that can scale it.

I lead the effort to systematize that work with AI agents -- taking the tacit, manual workflows behind EY's tax and consulting offerings and turning them into repeatable, agentic systems. Rather than building one monolithic model, we compose networks of specialized agents that encode domain knowledge, draw on authoritative sources, hand off to one another, and keep a human expert firmly in the loop for review and judgment. The point isn't to replace EY's professionals but to give each of them enormous leverage, so the firm's best thinking can be applied consistently to every client -- not just the ones lucky enough to draw the right expert. It's a problem that sits squarely at the intersection of genuinely hard AI engineering and a domain where the stakes, and the regulatory scrutiny, are about as high as they get, which is exactly the kind of work I love.

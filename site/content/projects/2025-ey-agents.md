EY is [one of the largest professional-services firms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_accounting_firms) in the world, and every year its tax and consulting practices deliver an astonishing volume of bespoke, high-stakes work. Almost all of it leans on the hard-won judgment of individual experts, but that also means the same problems get solved from scratch again and again. Their expertise is deep and meaningful, but difficult to leverage or repeat, and much of it lives in people's heads and in scattered documents rather than in systems that can scale it.

I led an effort to systematize elements of that work with AI agents into EY's [Global Tax Platform](https://www.ey.com/en_us/services/tax/global-tax-platform). We took tacit, manual workflows behind EY's tax and consulting offerings and turned them into repeatable, agentic systems. We composed networks of specialized agents that encoded domain knowledge, drew on authoritative sources, handed off to one another, and kept a human expert firmly in the loop for review and judgment with automated evaluations that run every time models or code are updated. It's a problem that sits squarely at the intersection of challenging AI engineering and a domain where the stakes, and the regulatory scrutiny, are about as high as they get.

This not only resulted in immense margin improvement, it also freed junior associates from a large amount of low-level drudgery and let them be deployed to much more strategic (and valuable) work. The point isn't to replace professionals but rather to give each of them enormous leverage, so the firm's best thinking can be applied consistently to every client, not just the ones lucky enough to draw the right expert.
